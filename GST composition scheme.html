CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION & THE PHILOSOPHY

When the Goods and Services Tax (GST) was launched, small business owners faced a nightmare. Filing 3 returns a month, matching Input Tax Credit (ITC) invoice-by-invoice, and maintaining complex accounting ledgers was impossible for a neighborhood grocery store or a small bakery.

Enter the Composition Scheme (Section 10 of the CGST Act).

Core Definitions

  • Composition Scheme: A simple and easy scheme under GST for taxpayers. Small taxpayers can get rid of tedious GST formalities and pay GST at a fixed, nominal rate of turnover.
  • Regular Taxpayer: Collects tax from customers, claims Input Tax Credit (ITC) on purchases, and pays the net difference to the government. Files monthly GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B.
  • Composition Dealer: Cannot collect tax from customers, cannot claim any ITC, and pays tax out of their own pocket as a flat percentage of their turnover. Files a simple quarterly statement.
  • Aggregate Turnover: The total value of all taxable supplies, exempt supplies, exports, and inter-state supplies of persons having the same PAN, computed on an all-India basis.

CHAPTER 2: ELIGIBILITY CRITERIA & TURNOVER THRESHOLDS

Not everyone can opt for this scheme. It is strictly reserved for small-scale operations.

2.1 The Standard Turnover Threshold

To be eligible for the Composition Scheme, the Aggregate Turnover of the business in the preceding financial year must not exceed ₹1.5 Crores.

(Note: If you have 3 branches in 3 different states under the same PAN, the turnover of all 3 branches is combined to check this ₹1.5 Crore limit).

2.2 The Special Category States Limit

For certain states, the turnover limit is reduced to ₹75 Lakhs.

These states are: Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Sikkim, Tripura, and Uttarakhand.

(Note: Assam, Himachal Pradesh, and J&K retain the ₹1.5 Crore limit).

2.3 Mandatory PAN-Level Compliance

You cannot play the system. If you have a clothing store and a hardware store under the same PAN, you cannot put the clothing store under the Regular scheme and the hardware store under the Composition scheme. Composition scheme is PAN-based, not GSTIN-based.

CHAPTER 3: APPLICABLE TAX RATES BY BUSINESS CLASSIFICATION

A Composition Dealer pays tax at a highly subsidized rate. However, the exact rate depends on the nature of your business.

  1. Manufacturers:
    • Rate: 1% (0.5% CGST + 0.5% SGST)
    • Calculated on: Total turnover in the State/UT.
  2. Traders (Dealers/Retailers):
    • Rate: 1% (0.5% CGST + 0.5% SGST)
    • Calculated on: Taxable turnover only. (If a grocery store sells taxable soap and tax-exempt loose wheat, the 1% is only calculated on the sale of the soap).
  3. Restaurants (Not serving alcohol):
    • Rate: 5% (2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST)
    • Calculated on: Total turnover in the State/UT.

CHAPTER 4: STRICT CONDITIONS & RESTRICTIONS (THE "CANNOT DO" LIST)

The government gives you a subsidized rate, but it takes away your trading freedom. If you violate any of these conditions, you will be thrown out of the scheme and heavily penalized.

  • No ITC (Input Tax Credit): You cannot claim a single rupee of GST paid on your purchases. It becomes a cost to your business.
  • No Tax Collection: You cannot charge GST from your customers. Your invoice cannot show CGST or SGST.
  • No Inter-State Sales: You cannot sell goods to another state. (However, you are allowed to buy/purchase goods from another state).
  • No E-Commerce Sales: You cannot sell goods through e-commerce portals like Amazon, Flipkart, or Swiggy if they are required to collect TCS. (Note: Recent amendments allow intra-state e-commerce supply for composition dealers, but inter-state is still banned).
  • No Non-Taxable Goods: You cannot sell goods outside the scope of GST (e.g., Petrol, Diesel, Alcohol for human consumption).
  • Certain Manufacturers Barred: Manufacturers of Ice Cream, Pan Masala, Tobacco, Aerated Waters, and Fly Ash Bricks cannot opt for this scheme.
  • No CTP/NRTP: Casual Taxable Persons and Non-Resident Taxable Persons cannot opt for this scheme.

CHAPTER 5: THE SPECIAL SCHEME FOR SERVICE PROVIDERS [SECTION 10(2A)]

Originally, the Composition Scheme was strictly for goods (with the sole exception of restaurants). Service providers (freelancers, salons, repair shops, consultants) protested.

In response, the government introduced a special scheme via Notification 2/2019.

Eligibility for Service Providers:

  • Your aggregate turnover in the preceding financial year must be up to ₹50 Lakhs.
  • You must not be eligible for the standard composition scheme (e.g., you provide services, or a mix of goods and services).

Tax Rate:

  • Rate: 6% (3% CGST + 3% SGST).
  • Calculated on: Total turnover.
Workshop Scenario: A graphic designer with an annual turnover of ₹35 Lakhs can opt for this scheme. They will not charge 18% GST to their clients; instead, they will pay 6% out of their own pocket and cannot claim ITC on their expensive computers and software.

CHAPTER 6: PORTAL NAVIGATION: OPTING-IN AND OPTING-OUT

You do not automatically get the composition scheme; you must apply for it.

6.1 Opting In (Form CMP-02)

To switch from Regular to Composition for the upcoming Financial Year, you must file Form CMP-02 prior to the commencement of the FY (usually by March 31st).

Step-by-Step:

  1. Log in to the GST Portal.
  2. Navigate to Services > Registration > Application to Opt for Composition Levy.
  3. Read the conditions, check the declaration box, sign via EVC/DSC, and submit.

6.2 Opting Out / Withdrawing (Form CMP-04)

If you cross the ₹1.5 Crore turnover limit midway through the year, or if you want to start selling inter-state, you must withdraw from the scheme within 7 days using Form CMP-04.

CHAPTER 7: INVOICING RULES: MASTERING THE "BILL OF SUPPLY"

A Composition Dealer cannot issue a Tax Invoice. Why? Because a Tax Invoice passes on Input Tax Credit to the buyer, and a composition dealer has no authority to do so.

Instead, you must issue a Bill of Supply.

Mandatory Requirements on a Bill of Supply:

  1. Must clearly state "Bill of Supply" at the top.
  2. Must not contain any column for CGST, SGST, or IGST amounts.
  3. The Legal Warning: At the top or bottom of the bill, you MUST print these exact words:
    “Composition taxable person, not eligible to collect tax on supplies.”
  4. Signage: You must display a signboard at your prominent place of business stating "Composition Taxable Person".

CHAPTER 8: RETURN FILING COMPLIANCE: CMP-08 & GSTR-4

The biggest advantage of the scheme is simplified compliance. No GSTR-1, no GSTR-3B.

8.1 The Quarterly Payment: Form CMP-08

Form CMP-08 is a simple statement-cum-challan. It is filed quarterly.

  • Due Date: 18th of the month succeeding the quarter (e.g., For April-June, due date is July 18th).
  • What it asks for: Just the total outward supply value and the tax payable (1%, 5%, or 6%).
[SCREENSHOT 5: "Form_CMP08_Dashboard.png"] (Workshop Instructor Note: Show the extremely simple CMP-08 screen. Highlight row 3: "Outward supplies (including exempt supplies)" and the auto-calculated tax columns).

8.2 The Annual Return: Form GSTR-4

You file only one return a year.

  • Due Date: 30th April following the end of the financial year.
  • What it asks for: Consolidated summary of turnover, inward supplies (purchases) from registered and unregistered dealers, and tax paid.

CHAPTER 9: SYSTEM SETUP: ACCOUNTING FOR COMPOSITION DEALERS

Accounting for a composition dealer requires a major shift in mindset for accountants used to the regular scheme.

9.1 Accounting for Purchases (Inward Supply)

Because you cannot claim ITC, the GST paid to your supplier becomes part of your inventory cost.

Scenario: Bought goods worth ₹10,000 + ₹1,800 GST.

  • Debit: Purchase A/c (or Inventory) ... ₹11,800
  • Credit: Supplier / Bank ... ₹11,800
(Note: There is NO 'Input CGST/SGST' ledger created).
[SCREENSHOT 7: "Tally_Purchase_Entry_Composition.png"] (Workshop Instructor Note: Show a Purchase entry in Tally configured for Composition. Highlight that the item's total cost absorbs the tax amount).

9.2 Accounting for Sales (Outward Supply)

Scenario: Sold goods worth ₹5,000 to a customer.

  • Debit: Cash/Customer A/c ... ₹5,000
  • Credit: Sales A/c ... ₹5,000
(Note: There is NO 'Output CGST/SGST' ledger).

9.3 Accounting for Quarterly Tax Payment (CMP-08)

At the end of the quarter, you calculate 1% (or applicable rate) of your total sales. This is a business expense.

Scenario: Quarterly sales = ₹10,00,000. Tax to pay = ₹10,000.

  • Debit: GST Expense (Taxes & Duties) A/c ... ₹10,000 (Hits P&L as an expense)
  • Credit: Bank A/c ... ₹10,000

CHAPTER 10: TRANSITIONING: NORMAL TO COMPOSITION & VICE VERSA

Businesses grow and shrink. Moving between schemes requires strict handling of Input Tax Credit on your existing inventory.

10.1 Normal to Composition (Form ITC-03)

If you switch to Composition, you lose the right to ITC. Therefore, you must reverse/pay back the ITC you previously claimed on the goods currently sitting in your warehouse as stock.

  • Calculate the exact ITC on raw materials, semi-finished, and finished goods in stock on the day before opting in.
  • Pay this amount or reverse it from your Electronic Credit Ledger using Form ITC-03 within 60 days.
[SCREENSHOT 9: "Form_ITC_03_Reversal.png"] (Workshop Instructor Note: Display the ITC-03 table where the taxpayer must declare the stock details and the corresponding tax amount to be reversed).

10.2 Composition to Normal (Form ITC-01)

If you cross ₹1.5 Crore, you are forced into the Regular scheme. The good news? You can now claim ITC on the stock currently in your warehouse.

  • File Form ITC-01 within 30 days.
  • Declare your stock. You must possess the purchase invoices (not older than 1 year). The government will credit this amount into your Electronic Credit Ledger.

CHAPTER 11: WORKSHOP FAQs, RCM, & COMMON PITFALLS

Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) under Composition

Crucial Warning: A composition dealer is NOT exempt from the Reverse Charge Mechanism.

If you hire a Goods Transport Agency (GTA) to bring inventory to your shop, you must pay 5% GST under RCM to the government. Furthermore, you cannot claim ITC on this RCM paid. It becomes a pure cost. You must declare this RCM liability in Form CMP-08.

Workshop FAQs

Q1: I am a composition dealer. Can a B2B customer claim ITC on the goods I sell them?
Answer: NO. Because you issue a Bill of Supply and do not charge tax, there is no tax for your customer to claim. This is why large corporations usually refuse to buy from Composition dealers.
Q2: I have a grocery store (Turnover ₹80 Lakhs) and I also earn ₹5 Lakhs from giving consulting advice. Can I opt for the 1% Composition Scheme?
Answer: Yes, under a specific relief clause. A person opting for the composition scheme for goods is allowed to provide services up to a value of 10% of turnover in the preceding FY or ₹5 Lakhs, whichever is higher. Since your service is exactly ₹5 Lakhs, you are safe.
Q3: Can I issue an E-way Bill if I am in the composition scheme?
Answer: YES. If the value of your consignment exceeds ₹50,000, you are legally required to generate an E-way bill. The portal allows composition dealers to log in and generate E-way bills against a Bill of Supply.
Q4: If my turnover crosses ₹1.5 Crore on October 15th, when do I start charging GST like a regular taxpayer?
Answer: Exactly on October 15th. The transition is instantaneous. From that exact invoice onward, you must issue Tax Invoices, charge GST, and file CMP-04 within 7 days.
Q5: What happens if I illegally collect GST from a customer while in the Composition scheme?
Answer: This is a severe offense under Section 73/74. You will be forced out of the scheme, face a penalty equivalent to the tax collected, and the entire tax collected must be deposited with the government immediately.
