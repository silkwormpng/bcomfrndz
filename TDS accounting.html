1. Introduction to the TDS Mechanism in India

1.1 The Genesis and Logic of TDS

Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) is a fundamental pillar of the Indian taxation system, governed by the Income Tax Act, 1961. Introduced with the principle of "Pay as You Earn" and "Collect as It is Earned," TDS ensures a steady flow of revenue to the Government while preventing tax evasion. Unlike self-assessment tax, which is paid by the earner, TDS places the legal onus on the payer (the deductor). When a specified payment (e.g., salary, professional fee, rent, contractor payment) is made, the payer is mandated to deduct a specific percentage as tax and remit it to the Central Government. The payee (the deductee) receives the net amount and claims the deducted tax as a credit against their final tax liability.

1.2 Understanding TAN (Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number)

To deduct or collect tax, every entity (except individuals/HUFs deducting tax under sections like 194M or 194-IB who can use their PAN) must obtain a TAN. Structure of TAN: 10-digit alphanumeric (e.g., DEL M 12345 L). First 3: city code; 4th: initial of deductor name; next 5: sequential numbers; last: check digit. Under Section 203A, quoting TAN is mandatory on all TDS returns, Challan 281, and TDS Certificates. Failure to apply for a TAN or not quoting it attracts a penalty of ₹10,000 under Section 272BB.

5. Year-End TDS Reconciliation Masterclass

GL to 26Q/24Q Tie-Out: Total credit in "TDS Payable" ledgers must match total tax deducted per Form 26Q/24Q filings.
Expense to TDS Base Value Check: Compare P&L expense vs base value declared in 26Q; explain variances (threshold, foreign payments, lower deduction cert).
Challan 281 to Bank Statement Match: Every debit in TDS Payable must correspond to a Challan 281 with BSR code, serial number, and date recorded in ERP.
Unreconciled Balances: March TDS deducted but payable by April 30 appears as current liability.

7. Navigating Major and Minor Heads

Assessment Year: For FY 2025-26, select AY 2026-27.
Major Head: 0020 (Income Tax on Companies) if deductee's PAN 4th char is 'C'; else 0021 (Other than Companies).
Minor Head: 200 (TDS payable by taxpayer) for regular deposits; 400 only for demand notices.
Nature of Payment: 92B(192) Salary, 94C(194C) Contractors, 94J(194J) Professional Fees, 94I(194I) Rent.

8. Executing Payment and Generating CIN

Enter tax breakdown (Income Tax, Surcharge, Cess, Interest, Fee 234E). Choose Net Banking or Payment Gateway → CRN generated. Upon success, Challan Identification Number (CIN) (20 digits: BSR code + date + serial number) is issued. Without CIN, TDS return cannot be filed.

9. Return Filing (Form 26Q & 24Q) – Data Preparation

Form 24Q: Salary TDS; Form 26Q: Non-salary resident payments; Form 27Q: Non-resident payments.
Pre-requisites: TAN, PAN, Challan details (CIN), Deductee PAN, amount paid, TDS amount, section code.
Use NSDL e-Gov RPU (free utility). Download latest version from tin-nsdl.com.

10. Validation, FVU, and Uploading the Return

RPU three tabs: Form Details, Challan Details, Annexure I (map deductees to challan IDs). Generate .csi file from e-Filing portal for challan validation. FVU cross-checks data; if successful, generates .fvu file + Form 27A. Upload .zip on Income Tax portal using DSC or Aadhaar OTP → get 15-digit Token Number.

11. Post-Filing Processing & TRACES

TRACES (tdscpc.gov.in) processes the return. Login with TAN → Statement Status → Token Number. Status: “Processed without Defaults” or “Processed with Defaults”. Defaults include short payment, short deduction, interest, late fee (234E). Download Justification Report for line‑by‑line error details.

12. Filing Correction Statements

Download Conso File from TRACES (password protected). Import into RPU, perform corrections: C1 (deductor details), C2 (challan), C3 (deductee details), C5 (PAN correction), C9 (add new challan). Re-generate .fvu and upload as correction return.

13. Form 26AS & The AIS Revolution

Form 26AS: Annual tax passbook showing TDS credit for deductee. If deductor fails to file return, credit does not appear.
AIS (Annual Information Statement): Shows SFT data (mutual funds, high-value spends), GST turnover, foreign remittances. For FY 2025-26, taxpayers must reconcile TIS (Taxpayer Information Summary) before ITR filing.

14. Reconciling Form 26AS / AIS with Books

Rule 37BA: TDS credit given to the person to whom income belongs, in the year income is assessable. Timing mismatch example: Invoice raised in March 2026 (FY 2025-26) but client deducts TDS in April 2026 (FY 2026-27) → TDS credit appears in 26AS of FY 2026-27. Claim credit via Schedule TDS in subsequent year. Identify mismatches using VLOOKUP between 26AS and “TDS Receivable” GL; errors due to wrong PAN, net booking, or non-compliance.

15. Issuing Statutory TDS Certificates (Form 16 / 16A)

Manual certificates illegal. Forms 16/16A must be downloaded from TRACES (unique watermark & logo).
Form 16 (Salary): Part A (TRACES) + Part B (salary computation, deductions).
Form 16A (Non-salary): Quarterly. Steps: TRACES → Downloads → Form 16A → select FY, quarter, PAN → KYC → request generated → download zip → use TRACES PDF Converter Utility to generate individual PDFs.

16. Finalizing Certificates & Digital Signatures

Bulk digital signing using USB token of Authorized Signatory (e-Mudhra, Signer.Digital). Signed PDFs auto-emailed to vendors/employees. Lower Deduction Certificate (Sec 197): Vendor applies via Form 13; deductor validates certificate on TRACES before applying lower rate.

17. The 15G & 15H Logic (Nil TDS Declarations)

Mathematical condition: Net Tax Liability = f(I) – Rebate ≤ 0. Eligible for zero TDS on interest/EPF withdrawal. Form 15G (individuals <60, specific income ≤ basic exemption), Form 15H (senior citizens ≥60, no specific income limit).

Eligibility: Resident individuals only; NRIs not eligible. For FY 2025-26, submit fresh forms at start of year via net banking (URN generated). Deductor (bank) must upload consolidated 15G/15H statement quarterly.

19. Penalties, Prosecutions & Compliance Conclusion

Section 234E: ₹200 per day late filing fee (capped at TDS amount). Section 271H: Penalty ₹10,000 to ₹1,00,000 for failure to file return within one year or incorrect information. Section 276B: Criminal prosecution (3 months to 7 years rigorous imprisonment) if TDS deducted but not deposited. Conclusion: Master TDS requires ERP, banking (e-Pay Tax), and RPU/FVU synergy; strict adherence to calendar and 26AS reconciliation shields from liability.
